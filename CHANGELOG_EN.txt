==========================================================================
CHANGES MADE RELATIVE TO THE ORIGINAL DIP MOD (Dreadnought Improvement Project)
==========================================================================
This file is updated continuously whenever a new data change is made.
Last update: see date at the end of the file.

Purpose: document EVERY change relative to the original (vanilla) DIP mod,
together with its justification — mostly historical, occasionally purely
gameplay/balance-driven (QoL). Every replaced table has the original kept
as a backup in the same folder under "<name>_backup_..._.csv".

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. GUNNERY — ARMOR PENETRATION (guns.csv, penetration.csv)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT: Rebuilt the armor penetration curves from scratch for all 21
calibers (1"-21") and all 5 "grades" (generations) of each caliber.

WHY: The original vanilla DIP values grew roughly LINEARLY with caliber
(e.g. +58mm per additional inch), which does not reflect real-world
penetration physics. In reality, penetration depends primarily on
muzzle/striking velocity and shell weight (via the classic De Marre
formula), not on caliber alone.

METHOD: Applied the De Marre formula:
    T/D = K(D) x D^1.07144 x (W/D^3)^0.71429 x V^1.42857
(T = penetrated armor thickness, D = caliber, W = shell weight,
V = striking velocity, in inches/pounds/ft-s)

The K(D) constant was calibrated against 6 REAL, documented penetration
tables from navweaps.com (US 8"/55 Mk12/15, German 28cm SK C/34,
US 12"/50 Mk8, UK 13.5"/45 Mk V, UK 15"/42 Mk I, US 16"/50 Mk7) — error
vs. real data is 0.5-13% across the whole range.
(The first calibration pass, based on only 2 data points at 15"/16",
produced errors of 2-35x for calibers far from that range — this was
corrected after the error was noticed.)

For each caliber and grade, a real historical gun was chosen as the
data source (shell weight, muzzle velocity) — the full source list is
in gun_sources_reference.csv. Calibers 1"-3" (never used against belt
armor in reality) and 17", 19", 21" (never actually built) are formula
extrapolations, not real combat tables.

ADDITIONAL FIX: The baseline curve (grade 5) across calibers was
smoothed (isotonic regression) to remove non-historical "dips" (e.g.
caliber 13-15" weaker than caliber 12" purely because the specific real
gun chosen for that generation happened to have a lower velocity).
Similarly, the grade multipliers (1-5) within a single caliber were
forced to be non-decreasing (G1 <= G2 <= ... <= G5), since the different
real guns used as a basis did not always improve monotonically.

EFFECT: small calibers (1-5") are weaker than the original (since they
historically never penetrated thick armor), medium 7-9" come out similar
or slightly stronger than the original (best-calibrated range), large
13"+ are weaker than the original (which scaled them up linearly with
no physical basis).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. GUN / TURRET WEIGHT (guns.csv: baseWeight, barrelW_1-5)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT: Barrel and turret weights recalculated using power laws calibrated
against 3 real, confirmed weights (US 16"/50 Mk7, UK 15"/42 Mk I,
Japanese 46cm/45 Type 94 from Yamato).

Barrel weight: W = 0.05564 x D^2.7732 (tonnes), extrapolation error ~4%.

FIX (turret weight change reverted): Turret structure weight (baseWeight)
was originally also recalculated using a power law: W = 1.528x10^-6 x
D^7.04. It turned out that with such a steep exponent, calibrated on
only 3 very large guns (16", 15", 46cm), the formula extrapolated down
to small calibers (1"-8") collapsed almost to zero (e.g. caliber 1" =
0.0 tonnes instead of 0.48t in vanilla DIP, caliber 5" = 0.127t instead
of 7.9t). In-game effect: small-caliber turrets had no weight budget
left for armor, so the player could not set any armor thickness on
those turrets at all. FIX: the baseWeight column in guns.csv was
restored to the original vanilla DIP values for all 21 calibers (the
power-law formula also proved unreliable outside its narrow calibration
range at the large end — e.g. at caliber 21" it gave roughly 6.5x MORE
than vanilla — so the entire turret-weight formula was reverted, not
just patched for small calibers). Barrel weight (barrelW_1-5) is
unchanged — a separate column, not affected by this issue. Backup:
guns_backup_przed_baseweight_fix.csv.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. GUN ACCURACY (guns.csv: accuracy_1-5)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
An attempt was made to recompute accuracy based on the documented 66%
reduction in US Navy gunnery dispersion from 1920-1945 (navweaps.com /
W.J. Jurens) — the result turned out too drastic (0.21-1.62) compared to
the original's narrow range (0.63-0.75), which the rest of the game's
hit-chance engine is tuned around. THIS WAS REVERTED to the original flat mod
values for all calibers.

4. SHIP TONNAGE LIMITS (technologies.csv)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT: Added missing tonnage caps that were absent from the original mod
(battleships could be built with no upper weight limit for the entire
game).

WHY — Washington Naval Treaty (1922) and 2nd London Treaty (1936):
- hull_strength_12 (year 1919): tonnage(bb;35000) — 35,000-ton limit,
  matching the 1922 Washington Naval Treaty (5:5:3 ratio for
  USA/UK/Japan, 35,000 long-ton cap per battleship).
- hull_strength_15 (year 1936): tonnage(bb;45000) — the "escalator
  clause" of the 1936-38 2nd London Treaty, raising the cap to 45,000
  tons after Japan declined to sign the treaty.
- hull_strength_end (year 1940): tonnage(bb;-1) — by 1940 the treaties
  had effectively lapsed (war had broken out), so the limit is removed.

CRITICAL FIX (corrupted CSV row — modern battleships unavailable despite
being researched): The hull_strength_15 edit described above (prepending
"tonnage(bb;45000), " to the already-existing "unlock(...)" text) had
originally been done incorrectly — the "effect" field in the CSV file
was not wrapped in quotes, and the added comma split it into TWO
separate columns instead of one. Result: the hull_strength_15 row ended
up with 15 columns instead of the correct 14, and the entire
"unlock(bb_6_kgeorge;bb_6_russia;bb_7_russia;bb_6_china;bb_6_littorio;
bb_7_littorio;bb_7_kgeorge;bb_7_austria;bb_7_china;bb_7_spain;
bb_6_soyuz;bb_7_soyuz;bb_8_china;bb_7_china_exp)" clause — i.e. the
unlock for ALL modern battleship hulls (King George V, Littorio, Soviet
Sovetsky Soyuz, Chinese, Austrian, Spanish) — landed outside the actual
"effect" column and was never actually applied by the game, even though
the technology showed as researched. FIX: the effect field was merged
back into a single correct string, "tonnage(bb;45000), unlock(...)" —
verified that no other row in the file has an incorrect column count.
Backup: technologies_backup_przed_naprawa_hull_strength_15.csv.
NOTE: on an already-running campaign where hull_strength_15 was
researched BEFORE this fix, re-researching that technology (or starting
a new game) may be needed — it is not certain the game engine
re-applies the effect of an already-"completed" technology without a
fresh calculation.

ALSO: hull_destroyer_27 (year 1935) had a jump in the destroyer tonnage
limit to 7,500 tons, which does not correspond to any real destroyer
class of that era (the largest WWII destroyers, such as the Japanese
Shimakaze or American Fletcher class, stayed within 2,000-3,500
standard tons). Changed to tonnage(dd;4200) — a reasonable ceiling with
headroom above the largest real destroyers of the era.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. TORPEDOES — OXYGEN PROPULSION / "LONG LANCE" (technologies.csv:
   torpedo_prop_4)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT: Raised the range bonus from oxygen propulsion from +40% to +100%
(torpedo_range_mod), and updated the description text from "14,000m at
40 Knots" to "35,000m at 40 Knots".

WHY: The Japanese Type 93 "Long Lance" (1935, oxygen propulsion instead
of compressed air) achieved 40,000m at 33-35 knots — roughly 3x more
than ANY other torpedo of the era at its longest setting (US Mark 15:
13,700m, UK Mark IX: 12,300m, German G7a: 12,000m). The original mod's
bonus value produced a maximum achievable range of roughly 14,000-
18,000m even at full tech development — about 2.2x weaker than reality.
The new value brings the max achievable range up to roughly 28,000-
35,000m, closer to the real Long Lance.

NOTE: The exact way the game engine sums percentage bonuses from
multiple technologies has not been fully verified (no access to source
code) — the actual in-game effect may differ from these calculations.
In-game testing and further tuning is recommended.

6. RESEARCH SPEED AND VARIANCE (params.csv)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
- research_progress_base: 3.0 -> 1 (percent) — lowered the base research
  progress rate ("100/x months to research a technology"), previously
  set well above vanilla DIP (0.8). This works together with the x10
  research cost (see below) to slow down inventions that were coming
  too fast.
- research_late_years_to_double: 12 -> 3 -> back to 12 -> finally 2
  years. The vanilla DIP value is 12; after a round of testing the
  final setting is 2 years — a technology "late" by just 2 years
  (very quickly) now gets doubled research speed, i.e. aggressive
  catch-up on technological backlog.
- tech_mod_randomness: 0.9 -> 0.5 — reduced the spread of each nation's
  hidden random "aptitude" for a given tech category from a 0.1x-1.9x
  range down to 0.5x-1.5x (still wider than vanilla's 0.7x-1.3x, but
  less extreme than the previous 0.9).
- Research cost/difficulty (the "cost" column in technologies.csv) was
  first ROLLED BACK to the level found in the archival "x4" file on
  disk, after it had previously been raised roughly 40x relative to
  that reference point during earlier experimentation.
  After testing in-game, the x4 level turned out to make inventions
  come too fast, so the research cost was RAISED AGAIN — this time to
  the level found in the archival "x10bak" file on disk, i.e. ten times
  the original mod's base cost. This change affects only the
  cost/difficulty column — the tonnage limits (section 4) and the
  torpedo range bonus (section 5) in technologies.csv are unchanged.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
6b. TOP RESEARCH-SPENDER BONUS (params.csv)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT: Reduced the research-speed bonus a nation gets for spending the
most on research relative to other players.

WHY: In earlier experimentation, this bonus had been
raised well above the original DIP mod's level (verified against the
mod's archived installer files), making the research leader's advantage
disproportionately large. To reduce the bonus for the research-spending
leader, both values were set
to EXACTLY THE MIDPOINT between the pre-change state and the original
vanilla DIP level:
- research_budget_mod_max ("highest possible research multiplier from
  budget difference to richest player"): 2.125 -> 1.625 (vanilla DIP:
  1.125)
- tech_budget_boost ("boost of research from maximum tech budget
  relative to average budget, percent, if above average"): 300 -> 205
  (vanilla DIP: 110)

UNCHANGED (deliberately left alone, since they concern the penalty for
under-funding research rather than the leader's bonus):
research_budget_mod_min (0.99), tech_budget_max (100), tech_budget_reduce
(60), tech_budget_curver (5.8).

7. ECONOMY AND AI FLEET BUILDING (params.csv)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROBLEM: After earlier AI parameter changes, some nations (e.g. the USA)
had nearly stopped building ships; later it was reported that NO nation
at all was building ships (zero fleets).
DIAGNOSIS: fleet_generation_money_min/max (per the file's own comment,
the higher these are, the FEWER ships get generated) were set to
0.35/0.2 — HIGHER than the original vanilla DIP (0.22/0.1), which
strongly suppressed fleet generation. An earlier attempt to fix this
(recorded earlier) was incorrectly logged as done, but
NEVER actually reached the device — the live file kept the old, too-high
0.35/0.2 values the whole time, so the problem kept coming back.
FIX (this time actually verified on the live file): fleet_generation_
money_min 0.35 -> 0.22, max 0.2 -> 0.1 — exactly the original vanilla
DIP values (restoring lower thresholds = more ships built by the AI).
Previous state backed up as params_backup_przed_naprawa_floty.csv.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. REBELLIONS AND INTERNAL UNREST (params.csv, governmentMod.csv,
   provinces.csv)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Rebellions were made more frequent and stronger,
and then GEOGRAPHICALLY DIFFERENTIATED:

a) params.csv (global rebellion strengthening):
   - goverment_changed_naval_funds_penalty: 0.75 -> 0.9
   - goverment_changed_penalty_decrease_per_turn: 0.05 -> 0.015
     (the penalty for changing government lasts longer)
   - revol_wealth_growth_add: -0.01 -> -0.025
   - revol_gdp_multiplier: -0.01 -> -0.025
     (revolutions/rebellions hit economic growth harder)
   - vp_to_respect: 0.00075 -> 0.0003 (harder to rebuild respect via
     victory points)
   - war_weariness_respect: 0 -> -3.5 (war weariness strongly reduces
     respect/stability)

b) governmentMod.csv: UnrestMod raised x1.5 for all 7 government types
   (e.g. absolute monarchy 2.75 -> 4.125).

c2) initial_cash_without_fleet_multiplier (params.csv): 0.24 -> 1.
   PROBLEM: even after fixing fleet_generation_money_min/max, some
   nations still built no ships at all at the start of the game.
   DIAGNOSIS: nations that start the game with NO fleet get an
   additional starting-cash multiplier of only 0.24, while nations
   that start with an existing fleet get 4 (initial_player_cash_
   multiplier) — roughly a 16.7x gap. This almost certainly put such
   nations' starting cash below the fleet_generation_money_min
   threshold from turn one, creating a loop: no fleet -> too little
   cash -> threshold never cleared -> still no fleet. This parameter
   was unchanged from vanilla DIP — likely an original flaw/design
   choice in the mod itself.
   FIX: raised to 1 (still clearly below the 4x multiplier for
   nations with an existing fleet, but above the threshold that was
   blocking the start).
   NOTE: the exact engine formula combining this multiplier with
   actual starting cash is not known from the CSV data alone —
   in-game testing is recommended; the value may need further tuning.

c3) cash_start_part (params.csv): 0.004 -> 0.02.
   WHAT: Raised the overall starting cash budget for ALL nations at
   once (not just fleet-less ones) — this parameter sets what fraction
   of a nation's yearly budget is paid out as starting cash. Increased
   5x.
   WHY: complements fix (c2) — more starting cash across the board
   reduces the risk of getting stuck below the fleet_generation_
   money_min threshold early in the game, regardless of whether a
   nation starts with an existing fleet or not.

ALSO FOUND ALONG THE WAY, PREVIOUSLY UNDOCUMENTED DEVIATIONS FROM
VANILLA (from earlier experimentation, now documented for completeness):
- cash_start_randomness: 0.05 (vanilla) -> 0.5 (current) — starting
  cash randomness spread raised 10x.
- shipyard_start: 12,500 (vanilla) -> 35,000 (current) — starting
  shipyard size raised roughly 2.8x.

c) provinces.csv (NEW — geographic differentiation): the revoltChance
   column was reduced by 65% only for provinces with isHome=1 (a
   nation's "home" provinces). Colonies and occupied territories
   (isHome=0) are left unchanged.
   WHY: In the (already previously modified) original data, home
   provinces had, on AVERAGE, a HIGHER revolt chance (6.99) than
   colonies (5.75) — which runs counter to historical logic (colonial
   and occupied-territory rebellions/uprisings, e.g. in India, Ireland,
   African colonies, were historically more frequent and severe than
   unrest in an ethnically homogeneous home territory). After the
   change: home provinces average 2.45, colonies/occupied territories
   remain at 5.75 — i.e. colonies/occupations are now clearly more
   unstable than one's own homeland, matching history.

9. NATION-SPECIFIC TECHNOLOGY SPECIALIZATIONS (aiPersonalities.csv)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
The file already contained a partial system of per-category research
speed bonuses per nation (TechMod). This was expanded and strengthened:

- JAPAN: torpedoes (size/tubes/propulsion) +30% -> +60%; added
  battleships (hull_strength) +30% and light cruisers (hull_cruiser)
  +30%. Rationale: the Japanese navy was famous for advanced torpedoes
  (Long Lance), rapid battleship modernization (Nagato, Kongo), and
  innovative light cruisers (Kuma, Nagara classes).

- RUSSIA (NEW): destroyers (hull_destroyer) +40%, armor quality/forging
  (armor_quality/forging) +30%, main guns (gun_main) +30%. Rationale:
  the Russian/Soviet tradition of large destroyer flotilla leaders, and
  the Sovetsky Soyuz-class project with very thick armor and 406mm
  guns — an emphasis on heavy artillery and tough armor.

- ITALY (NEW): main engines (engine_engine) +40%, boilers
  (engine_boiler) +30%, special engines (engine_special) +30%, light
  cruisers (hull_cruiser) +30%. Rationale: the Italian Regia Marina was
  famous for extremely fast ships — the Capitani Romani-class cruisers
  exceeded 40 knots, trading armor for maximum speed.

- SPAIN (NEW, more speculative): cruisers (hull_cruiser) +20%, gun
  mechanisms (gun_mech) +20%. Rationale: a more modest, defensively
  oriented fleet, built partly with British technical assistance (e.g.
  the España-class battleships).

- CHINA (NEW, more speculative): torpedo tubes (torpedo_tubes) +30%,
  destroyers (hull_destroyer) +20%. Rationale: a poor, technologically
  dependent navy favoring an asymmetric strategy of cheap torpedo forces
  as an "equalizer" against stronger opponents.

10. CONSIDERED BUT NOT IMPLEMENTED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Maximum belt armor thickness for bb 1940/1950 (genarmordata.csv):
  identified as potentially unrealistic (18" of armor), but no hard cap was
  introduced — armour thickness does not
  need to be hard-limited.
- Engine/machinery formulas (shipTypes.csv): the realism of engine
  room size/weight was checked, but changes were NOT made due to
  uncertainty about exactly how the game engine combines these values —
  risk of introducing an error without being able to fully verify it.
- Per-nation ballistics (penetration/velocity/shell weight): IMPOSSIBLE
  to achieve through CSV data editing alone. The game engine reads
  these values purely by caliber, with no "nation" field anywhere in
  guns.csv/penetration.csv/parts.csv. The same applies to technologies
  (no field restricting availability to a single nation). This would
  require changes to the mod's compiled code (TweaksAndFixes.dll), not
  just its data files.

11. "MODERN BATTLESHIP" HULLS — MINIMUM TONNAGE (parts.csv)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT: Lowered the tonnageMin field to 35000 tons for all 29 hull-type
rows whose nameUi contains "Modern Battleship" (variants I/II/III plus
the mission-specific "(Japan)"/"(USA)"/"(Germany)"/"(Italy)"/"(France)"/
"(Russia)"/"(British)" variants), across every nation: USA (bb_5_iowa,
bb_5_iowa_small, bb_usa_north_carolina_large), Japan (bb_5, bb_5_old),
Germany (bb_5_bismarck, bb_5_bismarck_small), France (bb_5_richelieu,
bb_5_france), Britain (bb_5_kgeorge, bb_6_kgeorge), Russia (bb_5_russia,
bb_6_russia, bb_6_soyuz), Italy (bb_5_italy, bb_6_italy, bb_6_littorio),
Austria (bb_6_austria), Spain (bb_6_spain), China (bb_6_china), plus the
matching mission variants. tonnageMax was left unchanged for every row —
only the lower bound was moved.

WHY: Researching hull_strength_13/14/15 unlocked these hulls in the tech
tree, but they could never actually be built — their tonnageMin
(originally 42500-66000 tons depending on variant) exceeded the current
battleship tonnage cap set by tonnage(bb;X) in technologies.csv (35000
tons after hull_strength_12, 45000 tons after hull_strength_15, no cap
at all until hull_strength_end in 1940). The result: the player could
only pick older "Modernized Dreadnought" hulls despite having fully
researched "Modern Battleship" technology — two independent gates (tech
and tonnage) were out of sync in time.

HISTORICAL NOTE: lowering to 35000 tons is actually CLOSER to reality
than the mod's original values. Real late-1930s fast battleships
displaced: South Dakota ~37,000 t standard, King George V ~36,000 t,
Bismarck ~41,700 t, Littorio ~40,700 t — none came close to the
56,000-66,000 tons the mod originally required as a minimum.

BALANCE RISK (knowingly accepted): "Modern
Battleship" hulls carry HIGHER fixed hull_form/stability/floatability
values than older hulls of the same tonnage class (e.g. bb_5_iowa: hull_
form 95 at any tonnage in its range), so at the same 35000-ton mark they
now outperform "Modernized Dreadnought" hulls statistically — some
power creep relative to the intended progression (bigger performance
should require a bigger hull), accepted deliberately so that researching
the technology has a real in-game effect instead of being a dead entry
in the tech tree.

Original backed up as: parts_backup_przed_tonnageMin_modern_bb.csv

12. THE SAME PROBLEM IN OTHER HULL CLASSES (parts.csv)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT: Every ship class (BB, BC, CA, CL, DD, TB) was systematically
checked for the same bug pattern: a hull unlocked by a technology but
with a tonnageMin exceeding that class's tonnage cap at the moment of
unlock. Result:

- BC (battlecruiser): NO problem — tonnage(bc;-1) (no cap) is set by the
  very same technology that introduces the BC class itself
  (hull_cruiser_45, 1907). The class and its uncapped tonnage appear
  simultaneously, so this conflict cannot occur there structurally.
- CA, CL, TB: checked, no rows found with tonnageMin exceeding that
  class's maximum cap.
- BB: the problem was much wider than just the "Modern Battleship" tier
  fixed earlier (see item 11). An additional 23 hulls were fixed,
  lowering tonnageMin to the tonnage cap in effect in the exact year
  their unlocking technology fires:
  * unlocked by hull_strength_12/13/14 (cap then: 35000 tons):
    bb_4 (46000→35000), bb_4_german (47000→35000), bb_4_japan
    (49000→35000), bb_4_japan_2 (51000→35000), bb_4_generic
    (54000→35000), bb_5_austria (64000→35000), bb_5_china
    (61000→35000), bb_5_japan (47000→35000), bb_5_exp_france
    (45500→35000) — the last two grouped with the "Super Battleship"
    tier below, since hull_strength_14 (1929) still operated under the
    35000-ton cap (45000 only arrives with hull_strength_15 in 1936);
  * the entire "Super Battleship" tier unlocked by hull_strength_14
    (1929, cap 35000): bb_6 (97000→35000), bb_6_iowa (88000→35000),
    bb_6_bismarck (84000→35000), bb_6_richelieu (70000→35000),
    bb_6_france (82500→35000), bb_7_bismarck (104000→35000);
  * the rest of "Super Battleship" unlocked by hull_strength_15 (1936,
    cap 45000): bb_7_kgeorge (80500→45000), bb_7_littorio
    (76500→45000), bb_7_spain (75500→45000), bb_7_china (74500→45000),
    bb_7_russia (85000→45000), bb_7_soyuz (96000→45000), bb_7_austria
    (92500→45000), bb_8_china (69000→45000).
- DD (destroyers): found a bug WORSE than BB's — PERMANENT, with no
  possibility of ever being fixed by further tech progress.
  hull_destroyer_27 (1935) is the LAST technology in the entire DD tree
  — it sets the tonnage cap to 4200 tons FOR GOOD (unlike BB, where
  hull_strength_end in 1940 removes the cap entirely, no later DD tech
  ever lifts it) — and in that same row unlocks 5 hulls exceeding that
  cap: dd_7_german_hybrid_dd ("Hybrid Destroyer", Germany/Austria,
  4450→4200), dd_8_hybrid_cruiser_gen ("Hybrid Destroyer", Spain/China/
  Scandinavia, 4750→4200), dd_8_hybrid_cruiser_italy ("Hybrid Destroyer
  II", Italy, 5050→4200), dd_8_france_dd ("Flotilla Leader", France,
  5250→4200), dd_8_russia_dd ("Flotilla Leader", Russia/Portugal/
  Netherlands/Greece, 5350→4200). Without this fix, these 5 hulls would
  have been unbuildable for the entire rest of the game, no exceptions.

METHOD: for each fixed hull, tonnageMin was set to the exact tonnage cap
in effect in the year its technology unlocks (not one flat number for
everything) — so the hull becomes buildable the moment it's researched,
matching the tech tree's own intended logic. tonnageMax was left
unchanged across all 28 rows.

BALANCE RISK: this compounds the effect noted in item 11 — the "Super
Battleship" tier now shares its lower tonnage floor (35000 or 45000)
with the "Modern Battleship" tier, despite representing much larger,
heavier designs (up to 130,000 tons at the top end). This follows
directly from the original tech tree's own cap curve (a step from 35000
to 45000 only in 1936, full removal only in 1940) — fixing that would
require redesigning the cap curve itself, which was out of scope here.

Original backed up as: parts_backup_przed_naprawa_super_bb_dd.csv

13. PHYSICAL LIMIT ON MAXIMUM "SUPER BATTLESHIP" TONNAGE (technologies.csv, parts.csv)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT: An upper tonnage limit of 200,000-240,000 t was set for the "Super
Battleship" tier, derived
PURELY from physical/engineering constraints (not treaties, cost, or
tactical sense), applied in two places:

1. technologies.csv, hull_strength_end (1940): changed the effect
   "tonnage(bb;-1)" (no cap at all) to "tonnage(bb;240000)" — the
   global BB tonnage ceiling is no longer infinite; it's now an
   explicit, physically-justified limit.

2. parts.csv: the tonnageMax field for all 14 "Super Battleship" tier
   hulls was proportionally stretched from its previous 78,500-130,000
   ton range to a new 200,000-240,000 ton range (preserving relative
   order and proportions between hulls/nations): bb_8_china
   (78500→200000), bb_7_china (82500→203000), bb_6_richelieu
   (85000→205000), bb_7_spain (87500→207000), bb_7_kgeorge and
   bb_7_littorio (92500→211000), bb_6_france (95000→213000), bb_7_russia
   (99000→216000), bb_7_austria (101500→218000), bb_6_bismarck
   (105000→221000), bb_6_iowa (109000→224000), bb_7_soyuz
   (123000→235000), bb_6 (125000→236000), bb_7_bismarck
   (130000→240000). tonnageMin was left unchanged (see item 12 — a
   separate, previously-fixed gate governing early buildability).

PHYSICAL RATIONALE (from research done before making the change): steel hull
strength is NOT a real constraint — the
largest tanker ever built (Seawise Giant/Knock Nevis) reached 564,763
DWT with no structural issues. The real historical limit was the
availability of a large enough dry dock: the German H-44 design (~131,000
tons standard / ~141,500 tons full load, 345 m long) was judged by its
own designers and the Kriegsmarine to be unbuildable on any slipway of
the era and purely speculative. The 200,000-240,000 ton figure chosen here
sits between that era-specific historical ceiling (~140,000 t) and
the scale of today's largest shipyard dry docks (700+ m, handling
200,000+ DWT ships) — representing "better infrastructure than the
1940s powers had, but not fully modern scale."

14. RADAR BONUS FOR NIGHT AND BAD WEATHER (technologies.csv)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT: Added two effects that were previously entirely absent to the three
radar technologies (`aim_rangefinder_11` [1935], `aim_rangefinder_12`
[1940], `aim_rangefinder_13` [1945]):
- stat(night;20 / 35 / 50) — the "night" stat (night visibility) has
  existed in stats.csv from the start ("Night Vision: Decreases
  visibility penalties during night"), but no technology anywhere in
  the entire tree ever granted it — ships never got any night-combat
  bonus at all, regardless of tech level.
- accuracy_weather(15 / 20 / 25) — additional bad-weather accuracy
  bonus. Previously accuracy_weather only grew until 1915
  (aim_control_6/8/9, +10 each) and then froze completely — the radar
  technologies (1935-1945) added nothing further here, despite radar
  historically having the biggest impact on accuracy in poor
  conditions.

WHY: The radar bonuses were checked for realism; conclusion: they should
be bigger specifically for night and bad weather. Checking the stats.csv
documentation: the "radar" field is
explicitly described as "unaffected from weather and night" — meaning
target DETECTION via radar already works regardless of conditions, but
the actual firing ACCURACY once a target is detected in those
conditions got no additional support from that same technology. This
was a genuine data gap, not just a balance question: the "night"
mechanic existed in the game engine (documented in stats.csv) but was
never actually used by any technology.

HISTORICAL RATIONALE: in the USS Washington vs Kirishima battle
(November 1942, a night engagement), the radar-equipped ship hit 6-9 of
75 shells (8-12%), while the opponent without radar, despite an
experienced night-fighting crew, hit maybe 1-2 shells on another ship
and none on Washington — the source (combinedfleet.com) sums it up as
"radar turned in a much better hit percentage than optics, even when
the optics were wielded by the best night fighters in the world."
Earlier fire-control radar could also track shell splashes out to about
20,000 yards, and an improved model (Mark 8 mod 3, roughly matching the
later technologies here) reliably out to 35,000+ yards, while optical
spotting deteriorated sharply beyond 18,000 yards.

VALUES CHOSEN (own values, not backed by a specific sourced figure —
no hard percentage statistics were found in available sources, only one
well-documented battle case): values escalate progressively with each
radar generation (20/35/50 for night, 15/20/25 for accuracy_weather),
mirroring the escalation pattern already present in these same rows for
other stats (e.g. aim: 150→165→180, stat(tspot;...): 150→200→220).

the player's). Each has a switch in UserData\MelonPreferences.cfg
[DIP_GG_Fixes].

A) Torpedo charge (LightShipTorpedoCharge = true). Postfix on
Ship.AILogic for an AI division leader of type tb, dd or cl with
torpedoes:
- when a visible enemy bb, bc or ca is closer than
  TorpedoChargeStartRange (12000 m), the division charges the nearest
  such target (Division.MoveTo on its position),
- the charge continues until the distance drops below torpedo range x

- the decision is re-evaluated every BattleAiThinkSeconds (4 battle
  seconds),
- divisions already retreating are left alone.

range of their strongest weapon (torpedoes) or run.

visible enemy ship within the gun group's range is damaged above
FocusFireMinDamage (0.15) and more than the target the game picked,
the guns switch to the more damaged target.

MelonLoader log: "[DIP GG Fixes][Battle] ...".

32. DIP_GG_Fixes 1.10.0 — ECONOMIC-COLLAPSE GRACE PERIOD, SHIP-LOADING GUARD; PENETRATION VS OKUN TABLES — 2026-10-12

A) lose_provinces_threshold DOES NOT EXIST IN THE GAME CODE. Checked in
GameAssembly.dll: the game's string literals contain no such key, so the
value in params.csv (DIP: 0.8; vanilla 0.3; EPFM 0.1) has no effect at
all. The row stays in the file unchanged (harmless).
What actually dissolves a nation (CampaignController.CheckForCampaignEnd,
Player.CanDissolve, Player.StateBudget):
- "dissolved due to economic collapse": when Player.StateBudget() <= 0,
  i.e. nation year income x (1 + wealth) x (1 - 0.05 x government-change
  penalty) x (blockaded: 1 - blockade_income_modifier) x transport factor
  x (revolution: 1 + revol_gdp_multiplier) reaches zero; in practice when
  wealth (accumulated GDP) falls to -100% or income to zero. Dissolution
  happens on the FIRST turn the condition holds, provided
  Player.CanDissolve() returns true;
- "dissolved due to losing all ports": no province with a port left;
- Player.CanDissolve(): always true for a nation never restored; for a
  restored one: true after turns_last_diissolve_nation turns, before that
  only if StateBudget <= 0;
- medium nations: separate CheckForMediumNationsDissolving — dissolved
  after losing all home provinces.
CORRECTION (DIP_GG_Fixes 1.10.0): new option EconomicCollapseGraceMonths
(default 24). A Player.CanDissolve prefix returns false while an AI
nation still owns ports and has been at zero state budget for fewer than
that many consecutive months; a gap in the series resets the counter.
Losing all ports dissolves at once as in vanilla. The player is not
affected. 0 = vanilla behaviour. The value 24 is an own
proposal. Log: "[DIP GG Fixes] <nation>: state budget at zero,
dissolution deferred ..." and "... grace period over ...".

C) Our penetration curves (penetration.csv row = grade 5 of the calibre,
without the ±7.5% barrel-length factor) against Okun/NavWeaps tables.
Vertical armour, deviation ours/real:
- 16" vs 16"/50 Mk7 (Mk8 2700 lb shell, FACEHARD): 0 km -12%, 9 km -15%,

39. DIP_GG_FIXES 1.11.0 — CHARGES BY RANGE AND SPEED, PLAYER SHIPS, VETERANCY, PANIC, CAPTURING SURRENDERED SHIPS — 2026-10-15
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Everything has a switch in UserData\MelonPreferences.cfg [DIP_GG_Fixes].
Takes effect on the next game start; campaign save format unchanged.

A. TORPEDO CHARGE START BY TORPEDO RANGE AND SPEED (all AI divisions).
   The start threshold used to be a per-nation constant (e.g. Russia
   9000 m), hence cases like CL Aurora starting at 8920 m against a
   battleship with 850 m torpedoes. Now: start = torpedo range x
   TorpedoChargeRangeMargin + (own speed - target speed, m/s) x
   ChargeApproachSeconds (240 s) + ChargeBaseExtra (2500 m), capped by the
   national chargeStartRange from battleDoctrines.csv (kept as the upper
   limit and national flavour).
B. AI CAPITAL SHIPS (bb/bc/ca) CHARGE OR CLOSE IN TOO:
   - with torpedoes and a speed advantage > 1.05x over the nearest enemy
     capital ship: torpedo charge like light ships (CapitalTorpedoCharge);
   - otherwise, when their guns are outranged (enemy range > 1.1x own) OR
     the main calibre cannot penetrate the target's belt at the current
     distance (Ship.GetPenetration vs target belt armour x PenetrationFactor):
     the division closes in until 0.7x own gun range or until penetration
     becomes possible (CapitalCloseInWhenOutgunned). Aborted above the
     doctrine's abortDamage. The first penetration/belt comparisons are
     logged for calibration.
C. PLAYER SHIPS:
   - TB/DD/CL divisions with torpedoes set as SCOUT (Division.IsScout) run
     the same torpedo charge as the AI, retreating when out of torpedoes or
     badly damaged (PlayerScoutCharge; log entries "[player scout]").
   - torpedoes are held while a friendly ship is in the lane to the torpedo
     target: the torpedo mode is temporarily set to Off and restored when
     the lane is clear (TorpedoFriendlyLane, lane half width 350 m, checked
     every 0.5 s). If the player changes the mode meanwhile, the mod backs off.
   - target selection (SmartTargeting) for gun groups without a manual
     target (Ship.Aim.manual = false), every 2 s: main guns -> most damaged
     enemy in range; on bb/bc the main guns pick among the enemies with the
     heaviest calibre (within 25 mm of the maximum); secondary guns -> the
     nearest torpedo threat (enemy with torpedoes closer than 1.5x its
     torpedo range), otherwise the most damaged. Manual orders take
     precedence. The same logic applies to the AI (SmartTargetingAi).
   CORRECTION: the 1.9 "focus fire" patch (postfix on Ship.FindBestEnemy)
   never ran - the game inlines FindBestEnemy into its callers (no calls in
   the native code), so the Harmony hook was never hit. Removed; replaced by
   SmartTargeting in a Ship.AimAndShoot postfix. The FocusFire /
   FocusFireMinDamage config keys remain without function.
D. NON-LINEAR VETERANCY (CrewLevelsRescale). The game keeps crew training
   levels in the crewTrainingLevels table (accuracy, aiming, reload, damage
   control) and interpolates between neighbouring levels by training points.
   After data load the table is rescaled: baseline = the level with values
   closest to 1.0; deviation r in [-1, +1] from the baseline; effect =
   sign(r) x |r|^1.6 x (+1.0 for better, 0.5 for worse), i.e. elite +100%,
   worst crew -50%, intermediate levels change little (positive effect 2x
   stronger than negative - author's decision). The direction of each stat
   is taken from vanilla (time-like stats scaled by the reciprocal). Vanilla
   and rescaled values are written to the log at start.
E. SURRENDER BY TRAINING (SurrenderByTraining). Vanilla:
   crew_percents_surrender_threshold = 0.55 for everybody. Now the threshold
   is interpolated from 0.70 (worst crew) to 0.40 (best) by training rank;
   injected through Param during Ship.CheckForSurrender.
F. PANIC (CrewPanic) - AI and player. Every 3 s a ship with training rank
   < 0.4 and damage > 0.5 or crew < 0.7 has a chance (0.2 x (0.5 + excess)
   x (0.3 + 0.7 x "greenness")) to break: the division retreats, or, if the
   ship is not the leader, it detaches (DetachShips) and retreats alone. Once
   per ship per battle, log entry "PANIC:". Divisions already retreating and
   surrendered ships are skipped.
G. CAPTURING SURRENDERED SHIPS (CaptureSurrenderedShips) - campaign.
   Prefix on BattleManager.DamageShip (the game calls it after the battle
   for every ship with its damage). If the ship has IsSurrendered and the
   opponent still has at least one working ship in the battle:
   CampaignController.TransferShipToNewOwner to the winner (the same
   function the game uses for sales and treaties: clones the design, assigns
   a port), crew training points reset (green crew), damage set to
   CaptureDamage 0.6 so the game sends the ship to repair in the new owner's
   port. The first 30 ships after a battle are logged with the surrendered
   flag to verify that the flag survives to this point.
   Precedent: Tsushima 1905 (Nebogatov's ships in Japanese service).
   STATUS: untested in game - needs a battle with a surrender.
RISKS: B and C.3 affect every ship in battle - on problems switch the
relevant key off. SmartTargeting counts target changes ("[N changes]" in the
log); if the game restores its own target every frame the counter will grow
fast - then the design needs rework.

65. IRON ADMIRAL 1.2.16 (DIP_GG_FIXES 1.11.31) — SERIES PRODUCTION, INFLATION AND CREDIT, TREASURY PANEL, IN-GAME HELP, CAMPAIGN GUIDE — 2026-10-18
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY: in the base game every ship of a class costs the same and takes the
same time, and money has no time value: loans, printed money, reparations
and unspent cash have no effect on prices. Historically the first ship of a
class was the expensive one (design changes, new tooling), follow-on ships
became cheaper and faster along a learning curve (Wright 1936; navy
programmes such as the British "Town" class and the US Fletcher-class
destroyers built dozens of hulls in parallel), idle yards bid low and full
yards charged more. Large borrowing and money printing drove inflation,
lenders priced in a country's credit and inflation eroded debt.
WHAT - series production (all nations, construction and refits):
- Prototype (first ship of a design): cost x1.15, time x1.10.
- Series law only for ships laid down after a ship of the design was
  completed; sisters ordered while the prototype is still on the slip are
  built at base cost and time. The laying-down month and whether the
  prototype was already in service are recorded when the order is placed
  (the game's own ship date is the design date, not the laying-down date);
  ships laid down before this version get the month estimated from their
  building progress. The record is kept with the economy state.
- Learning curve: cost 94% per doubling of the series (floor x0.80), time
  90% per doubling, building time never below 2 months.
- Batch: two or more ships of a design laid down within 6 months: cost -5%.
- Common dock: ships of one design laid down together that fit side by
  side into the largest dock: time x m^log2(0.8) (m = ships fitting
  together). The prototype gets neither batch nor dock bonus.
- Shipyard occupancy below 30%: cost x0.90, time x0.95; above 90%: cost
  x1.10, time x1.10.
- Sister ships in service: maintenance -3% each, at most -15%.
- 36 months without a new ship of the design: the next one is a prototype.
- Example (author's campaign, 1910-1911): destroyer V-1, base 7 months;
  29 sisters ordered together after V-1 was commissioned: time x0.19-0.29
  (all limited to 2 months), cost x0.68-0.80.
WHAT - inflation and credit (great powers):
- Inflation impulse relative to the economy (GDP = state budget): 0.4
  points per 1% of annual GDP of extraordinary inflow (loans, grants,
  reparations, loot, sales), 1.2 points for printed money; spending
  changes act symmetrically (0.4 points per 1% of GDP, monthly change).
- Inflation raises all expenses of the nation; it returns to a 1% baseline
  (6% of the distance per month); research slows inflation only down to the
  baseline (diminishing returns), so deflation comes only from large
  spending cuts; range -15%...300%.
- Loan interest = inflation + spread 2-5 points by credit rating (debt /
  GDP, worst at 50%; +0.5 deficit, +0.5 war; 15 years after a bankruptcy

  Ratings AAA...CCC. Inflation erodes the loan balance and the overdraft.
- Unspent cash earns inflation minus 1.5 points a year (never below 0).
  The first test build paid 150% of inflation; with printed money and 51%
  inflation that meant about 77% a year on the printed cash.
- Research and shipyard expansion add GDP growth (diminishing returns /
  relative to the expansion size); inflation above 10% and sharp jumps add
  unrest.
- The treasury crisis (war loan, printing, cuts) and balance-of-power
  grants feed the same model; economy state saved per save game in

66. IRON ADMIRAL 1.2.17 — WHAT INFLATION MEANS: SURCHARGE, NOT A PRICE LEVEL (DOCUMENTATION) — 2026-10-18
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY: in the test campaign Britain's budget (GDP) rose from 30.2 to 39.3 bn
in two turns while its inflation fell from 33% to 29%. The question was
whether GDP grows with inflation. It does not: the mod treats inflation as
a surcharge at the current yearly rate on all expenses; prices do not
accumulate into a permanent price level, and the state budget is not
inflated (every figure in today's money, a "real" model). A nominal price
index (prices and budgets growing together for good) was considered and
rejected: it would push ship prices and budgets out of the scale the game
is built for after a few years of high inflation, with no gain in play.
The swing in Britain's budget comes from the game itself (war, peace,
recovery after the crisis).
WHAT: explanation added to the in-game Help (chapter "Inflation, credit and
growth", English and German .lng) and to GUIDE_EN.md; descriptions updated.
DIP_GG_Fixes.dll unchanged (1.11.31).
STATUS: published as Iron Admiral 1.2.17.

67. IRON ADMIRAL 1.2.18 (DIP_GG_FIXES 1.11.32) — AI SHIPYARD EXPANSION ONLY ON A STEADY OR LARGE SURPLUS — 2026-10-18
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY: the rule from section 60 (no expansion with a deficit) checked only
the current month. In the test campaign (October 1911) Britain had one
positive month (+86M after its research budget was cut), started a
shipyard expansion at once (about 370M a month) and was 310M a month in
deficit the next turn, with cash falling by about 300M per turn. The USA
earlier paid 317M a month for an expansion on a 422M monthly budget.
WHAT: an AI great power starts a shipyard expansion only when
- it has had a surplus for 6 months in a row (AiShipyardSurplusMonths) and
  enough cash to cover the expected shortfall for the whole expansion
  (expected monthly cost minus surplus, times its length), or
- its monthly surplus covers 1.25 x the expected monthly expansion cost
  (AiShipyardCostCover).
The expected cost and length are remembered from the nation's last
expansion; before one is seen, half the monthly budget is assumed. The old
limits stay (no expansion with a deficit or with capacity above 2 x the
fleet). The surplus counter is not saved: after loading, the first 6
months allow an expansion only through the large-surplus rule. The log
shows the surplus, months in a row, expected cost and missing cash when an
expansion is refused. Player not affected.
STATUS: published as Iron Admiral 1.2.18.

69. IRON ADMIRAL 1.2.20 (DIP_GG_FIXES 1.11.38) — COALITION WARS AGAINST A DOMINANT POWER, NO AI DESIGNS WITHOUT A WAY TO BUILD — 2026-10-18
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY: in the test campaign (1913-1914) Germany had attitude -99 with almost
every great power and no one declared war. The war diagnostics (section 68)
showed why: each hostile power alone had at most half of the German fleet
(USA x0.42-0.51, Britain x0.33-0.52, Japan x0.34, Italy x0.25) and the game
keeps relations at -99 until a nation judges a war worth it; together they
had about 1.1-1.6x. Historically the dominant naval power faced coalitions
(Britain's balance-of-power policy, the Entente against Germany).
WHAT - coalition wars (every nation, player and AI alike):
- Hostile powers of a target: AI great powers in "almost war" with
  attitude at or below -90 (CoalitionAttitude), plus those already at war
  with it.
- The target must be dominant: its fleet power is greater than that of
  every single hostile power, and the hostile powers together reach 1.5x
  its fleet power (CoalitionPowerRatio).
- When nobody fights the target yet, the strongest eligible member
  declares war with a 25% chance per turn (CoalitionStartChance); once a
  member is at war, every other eligible member joins with 50% per turn
  (CoalitionJoinChance).
- Eligible: AI only (the player decides his own wars), cash not below zero,
  no bankruptcy mark, fewer than 3 wars (CoalitionMaxWars).
- The war is started through the game's attitude change; when the game
  keeps the relation at -99 the attitude is set to -100 directly. Confirmed
  in game: the game and TweaksAndFixes treat such wars as wars (war checks,
  peace talks).
- The first 1.11.37 build lacked the dominance test and piled AI powers on
  the weak Netherlands (coalition x140-x207 of its fleet); 1.11.38 adds it.
WHAT - AI ship designs: an AI nation skips generating new designs in a
turn when it has no ports, cash below zero, no shipbuilding capacity or
full shipyards (AiDesignOnlyWhenBuildable); designs at campaign start are
not affected. Log: "[Design] <nation>: no new ship designs - <reason>"
(confirmed: Scandinavia, 84,469 of 73,249 t under construction).
Log: "[War] coalition against <target> ready (...)" and "[War] <nation>
leads / joins ... war: True".

70. IRON ADMIRAL 1.2.21 (DIP_GG_FIXES 1.11.40) — REFITS OF SHIPS ABOVE TREATY LIMITS, REACHABLE TORPEDO CHARGES, NO HOPELESS CLOSE-IN, EVASIVE ZIGZAG — 2026-10-18
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY - refits: in the test campaign (Germany, 1916) battleships built before
the Washington Treaty limits were above the treaty tonnage and could not be
refitted at all: the refit design was checked against the treaty limit like
a new ship. The treaty itself (1922, Chapter II Part 3 Section I(d))
allowed existing capital ships to add up to 3,000 t for anti-aircraft,
anti-torpedo (bulges) and deck protection.
WHAT - refits: for a refit design the tonnage limit is raised to the ship's
own tonnage before the refit plus 3,000 t (RefitTreatyAllowanceTons), never
above the hull's own maximum; new ships keep the treaty limit. Log:
"[Refit] <ship>: refit tonnage limit X t -> Y t". 0 = vanilla.
WHY - battle AI (battle of February 1916, 3 German battleships against 40
US ships, log analysis):
- 20 torpedo charges, several of them impossible: CL Omaha charged from
  14.6 km with 1.6 km torpedoes at 4.3 against 12.1 speed units; DD Tolman
  from 8.7 km with 1.7 km torpedoes. A division that could not escape went
  straight into a charge without checking whether it could reach the target.
- 19 close-ins by cruisers and a battleship with 76-466 mm penetration
  against a 634 mm belt; every close-in ended "still cannot penetrate,
  still outranged" - the ships only sailed into the fire.
WHAT - battle AI:
- Charge reach: a division that cannot escape starts a torpedo charge only
  when the target is within its charge start distance x1.5
  (ChargeReachFactor) or when it is faster than the target; otherwise it
  fights with guns. Log: "charge on <target> not feasible (...)".
- No hopeless close-in: before closing in, penetration is checked at the
  closest planned range (own gun range x CloseInStopRangeFactor); when the
  belt could not be penetrated even there, the ship does not close in
  (CloseInOnlyIfPenetrates). Log: "cannot penetrate <target> even at X m".
- Evasive zigzag (EvasiveManeuvers): during a torpedo charge (until 1.3x
  torpedo launch range) and during a close-in while out of own gun range or
  without penetration, AI divisions alternate their course by +-30 degrees
  (EvasiveAngle) in legs of about 25 s, randomised +-30%
  (EvasiveLegSeconds). The game's accuracy model penalises fire at a
  turning target (params.csv acc_turning_target -0.5 at 2.5 deg/s) but
  penalises the turning ship's own fire even more (acc_turning_self -0.8),
  so the zigzag is used only while the ship's own guns are not effective.
  The final approach to torpedo launch range stays straight. Log: "evasive
  zigzag +-30 deg while charging / closing in".
STATUS: published as Iron Admiral 1.2.21; in-game confirmation of the refit
limit and of the battle changes pending.

72. IRON ADMIRAL 1.2.23 (DIP_GG_FIXES 1.11.49) — MASSED TORPEDO ATTACK, RAMMING, HOPELESS BATTLESHIPS RETREAT, ESCAPE MISSIONS, TORPEDO TURN-AWAY, HISTORICAL GUNNERY DATA — 2026-10-19
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY - battle AI: a single destroyer or light cruiser that cannot reach
torpedo range alive is right not to charge (section 70), but a whole
flotilla attacking together is a different matter: the enemy cannot sink
every ship before some of them reach launch range - the reasoning behind
massed destroyer attacks in the First World War. The same logic was missing
for the other end of the scale: an AI battleship that could not penetrate
its opponent even at close range simply sat in the battle, and a task force
whose task was to get away fought to the last ship.
WHAT - battle AI (AI only, player ships are not affected):
- Massed torpedo attack (FleetTorpedoAttack): while the enemy has more
  battleships and battlecruisers afloat than the AI side, every AI torpedo
  division charges the enemy capital ships at once, whatever the distance;
  the charge still breaks off on heavy damage or when the torpedoes are
  spent. Log: "massed torpedo attack ON for <nation>: enemy battleships/
  battlecruisers X vs own Y", "<ship>: massed torpedo attack - charges ...".
  Seen in the battle of 19 October 1916 (4 German battleships against 9 US
  light cruisers): all US cruisers went in, two were taken as prizes.
- Ramming (Ramming, RamRange 2000 m): an AI light ship with no torpedoes
  left that cannot escape, or takes part in a massed attack, steers at the
  nearest visible enemy ship within ramming range, aiming a little ahead of
  it. Log: "<ship> rams <target> at X m".
- Hopeless battleships retreat (CapitalEscapeWhenHopeless): an AI
  battleship or battlecruiser that cannot penetrate its target even at the
  closest planned range (section 70) retreats when it can survive the
  battle - it is faster than every visible enemy, or no visible enemy can
  penetrate its own belt. A retreat on armour alone is not cancelled by the
  "cannot outrun - fight" rule. Log: "cannot hurt <target> and can survive
  the battle (...) - retreats to save the ship".
- Escape missions (EscapeMissionLogic, EscapeBattleTypes =
  escape_taskforce): in battle types where the defending side's task is to
  get away there is no massed attack; a division that is faster than its
  pursuers retreats, a division that cannot outrun them splits into single
  ships that flee on different bearings (fanned out up to 75 degrees from
  the direction away from the enemy), so that part of the force escapes -
  as a commander would scatter a force that cannot outrun its pursuers.
  This rule takes precedence over the other AI rules. Log: "escape mission
  - ... retreats instead of attacking", "... the division scatters".
  Prompted by the battle of 19 October 1916: in a "Task Force fails to
  escape" battle the whole US cruiser force charged from more than 30 km.
- Turning away from torpedoes (TorpedoTurnAway, TorpedoTurnAwayRange
  4000 m, TorpedoTurnAwaySeconds 45 s): an AI division that is not itself
  charging and sights enemy torpedo tracks that would pass within 500 m of
  one of its ships turns to run parallel to them (combing the tracks, the
  standard counter used by the Grand Fleet at Jutland). Log: "N enemy
  torpedo track(s) sighted ... division turns away".
- Settings panel (section 71): opening it no longer disables the game's
  event system; the TweaksAndFixes campaign camera reads it every frame and
  threw an error per frame while the panel was open.
- Log: a massed charge reads "massed attack" instead of a meaningless
  start distance.
WHY - data: comparison with the NavWeaps gun data and with Okun's ricochet
tables.
WHAT - data:
- SAPC shells (technologies.csv projectile_shell_ia_sapc, components.csv
  ap_6, 1925): semi-armour-piercing capped shells - more bursting charge and
  damage (ap_damage +50, ap_fire +12), less penetration (-20), ricochet
  angle modifiers 10 / 25 and a shorter fuze (-0.5); names in
  English.lng and German.lng.
- Rates of fire (guns.csv firerate by grade), after NavWeaps: 6" grade 1
  4 -> 5; 8" grades 4-5 5 / 5.5 -> 4; 11" grades 2-4 2.07 / 2.45 / 2.85 -> 3
  (German 28 cm SK L/45 and L/50 of 1907-1913: 3 rounds/minute nominal);
  12" grade 5 3.35 -> 3.0; 14" grades 4-5 2.1 / 2.5 -> 2.0; 16" grades 3-4
  1.72 / 1.95 -> 1.5.
- Ricochet (params.csv): ricochet_angle_from 30 -> 45, ricochet_angle_to
  50 -> 80, ricochet_chance 55 -> 100 - after Okun's model (M79): uncapped
  shells enter the ricochet region above 45 degrees and always ricochet
  above 80 degrees.
- Destroyer secondary guns (shipTypes.csv dd): secondary calibre range 1-2"
  (was 2-2) and ai_max_caliber_sec(2) (was 0), so AI destroyer designs carry
  light secondary guns.
WHAT - diagnostics: armour diagnostics in the log (ArmorDiagnostics, log
only): the game's penetration functions return the same value at every
target aspect (0-90 degrees), so aspect does not reduce penetration; deck
hits do occur (side-hit chance 81% at 0-15 degrees falling to 26-39% at
45-75 degrees in the first battle measured); per-battle summary "[Armor] battle summary". The hit-type
counter also counts interface calls and is not a count of real hits.
WHAT - save compatibility check: built in 1.11.42 and withdrawn in 1.11.46.
Its hooks on the loading of every ship record (Ship.FromStore,
Player.FromStore) made every ship record fail to load, even when nothing
was changed; the tool stays switched off until it is rebuilt on another
hook. Saves themselves were not affected once it was removed.
STATUS: published as Iron Admiral 1.2.23; massed attack confirmed in game,
ramming, battleship retreat, escape-mission logic and the torpedo turn-away
pending confirmation in battle.

73. IRON ADMIRAL 1.2.24 (DIP_GG_FIXES 1.11.50) — AI TACTICS BY SHIP SIZE, SURRENDERED SHIPS TO THE BATTLE WINNER, SERIES PRODUCTION FIXES — 2026-10-19

WHY - battle AI: the battle of 19 October 1916 (Coastal Raid, 1 German
armoured cruiser and 1 destroyer against 1 Scandinavian armoured cruiser,
4 light cruisers and a transport) showed that the massed attack, the torpedo
targets and the retreat of a ship that cannot hurt its enemy all depended on
battleships being present. Without battleships on either side the rules did
nothing, although the same reasoning applies to any fleet.
WHAT - battle AI (AI only):
- Massed torpedo attack (FleetTorpedoAttack): switches on when the enemy's
  combat tonnage in the battle (transports excluded) exceeds the AI side's

  applies to the main ships of a side - battleships and battlecruisers, and
  any other non-light ship of at least 75% of the tonnage of the largest own
  ship in the battle (MainShipSizeRatio). Without battleships the armoured
  cruisers take that role.

when its own side won the battle; in the same battle a surrendered German
destroyer was lost although the Scandinavian force was destroyed.
WHAT - prizes (SurrenderedShipsToBattleWinner): surrendered ships go to the
side that won the battle (the game's battle winner, otherwise the side with
more victory points). A ship whose own side won returns to its owner and
goes to repair with the same damage as a prize and a minimum crew, crew
training kept. A ship whose side lost is captured as before. A drawn battle
keeps the previous rule. Log: "[Capture] ... surrendered but <nation> won the
battle (...) - ship returns to its owner".
WHY - series production: 13 cruisers of one class ordered in one session
were each logged as the second ship of the series with no batch or dock
effect, and 6 of them had been scrapped before completion.
WHAT - series production:
- A ship scrapped or sunk before completion no longer counts towards the
  series (learning curve, batch, common dock).
- The series log line of a ship is written again whenever its series
  number, batch, common dock or shipyard-occupancy band changes; orders
  placed during one session are counted together at the end of the turn.
- Confirmed in game: AI navies ordering in the same turn get the learning
  curve, batch discount and common-dock time (e.g. 7 destroyers in one
  dock).
CONFIRMED in game (section 72): turning away from torpedoes (Scandinavian
armoured cruiser, track passing 57 m and 231 m away); slow ships leaving
the division and fighting when they cannot outrun the enemy; charges
aborted on damage.
