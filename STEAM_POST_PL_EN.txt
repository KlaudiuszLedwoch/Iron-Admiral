[b]What is new today (19 October 2026) - Iron Admiral 1.2.25, DIP_GG_Fixes 1.11.51[/b]
[list]
[*][b]Enemy tactics no longer need battleships:[/b] the size of the ships in the battle decides, not their class. Cruiser and destroyer fights now get massed torpedo attacks, torpedo runs and withdrawals too.
[*][b]A massed torpedo attack - only when it can work:[/b] when you bring the stronger force and the enemy has enough torpedo craft for your ships, they all go in together. One destroyer alone would not get through - part of a whole flotilla does. A handful of boats no longer throws itself at your battle line.
[*][b]An outmatched enemy leaves:[/b] when your force is too strong and the enemy has too few torpedo craft to attack, its ships that are faster than yours withdraw instead of waiting to be sunk. Slower ones fight on.
[*][b]Torpedoes at anything bigger:[/b] enemy destroyers go for your cruisers, light cruisers go for your transports - any target clearly larger than the attacker.
[*][b]Main ships that cannot hurt you leave:[/b] the largest enemy ships - battleships, or armoured cruisers when no battleships are present - withdraw when they cannot penetrate your armour even at close range and can survive the battle.
[*][b]Surrendered ships go to the winner:[/b] if one of your ships surrenders but you win the battle, it comes back to you damaged instead of being lost. If the enemy wins, it takes the ship as a prize.
[*][b]Series production fixes:[/b] ships scrapped before completion no longer count towards a series, and the log reports the real series effects after the turn ends.
[*][b]Ramming, escape missions, turning away from torpedoes:[/b] enemy light ships with no torpedoes left that cannot escape ram; a task force whose job is to get away runs or scatters; divisions that spot torpedo tracks turn to run parallel to them.
[*][b]Settings panel in the game (F10):[/b] every feature of the mod with an ON/OFF switch and a short description. Changes apply at once and are saved.
[/list]

[b]18 October 2026 - Iron Admiral 1.2.14-1.2.21[/b]
[list]
[*][b]Series production is live:[/b] the first ship of a new design is an expensive prototype. Ships laid down after the prototype is in service get cheaper and faster the longer the series runs; ordering a class together pays off, and small ships that share the slips come out in a fraction of the time. Sister ships are cheaper to keep in service, an idle shipyard takes orders on better terms than an overloaded one, and refits follow the same rules - for you and for the AI.
[*][b]How to order a new class:[/b] build one ship first and wait until it is commissioned, then order the rest together. From the test campaign: a destroyer that takes 7 months as a prototype - its 29 sisters, ordered together after the prototype entered service, were built in a fraction of that time and markedly cheaper. Ordered all at once, they would all have cost full price.
[*][b]Inflation and credit:[/b] borrowing, printing money and sudden windfalls drive prices up - the larger compared with your economy, the stronger; spending cuts bring them down, and without new shocks prices settle back to normal. Loans cost inflation plus a margin set by your credit rating (debt, deficit, war, past bankruptcy), inflation eats into old debt, and a cash reserve keeps its value but will not make you rich. Runaway inflation stirs unrest.
[*][b]Treasury information in the game:[/b] a treasury panel on the campaign map (F8) with inflation and its causes, credit rating, interest, growth, ships under construction and the other great powers; inflation back in the Finances window; a monthly treasury report (can be switched off) and warnings for important events.
[*][b]In-game Help:[/b] new chapter "Iron Admiral - new mechanics". [b]Campaign guide:[/b] GUIDE_EN.md in the package and on GitHub, with examples from the test campaign and player reports.
[*]Existing campaigns continue; ships already under construction switch to the new rules.
[*][b]What inflation means:[/b] a surcharge on everything you pay for as long as it lasts, not a price level that keeps growing - when inflation falls, prices fall back, and budgets stay in today's money, so a bad spell never leaves the game's numbers out of scale.
[*][b]AI expands its shipyards only when it can afford it:[/b] one good month is no longer enough - an AI power needs a steady surplus and cash in hand, or a surplus large enough to carry the expansion.
[*][b]Fixes:[/b] refits ordered together pay the prototype price only once; no more stray ship parts on the world map after the refit screen; long-running classes no longer restart as prototypes.
[*][b]Coalitions against the dominant power:[/b] grow too strong at sea and the powers that hate you gang up - one of them strikes first, the others follow. Works against AI empires too.
[*][b]Leaner AI turns:[/b] AI nations stop drawing new ship designs while they cannot build them (no ports, no money or full yards).
[*][b]Refits of ships above the treaty limits:[/b] battleships built before the naval treaties can be modernised again - a refit may add a limited amount of tonnage above the ship's current size, as the Washington Treaty allowed for anti-aircraft, anti-torpedo and deck protection; new construction keeps the treaty limit.
[*][b]Smarter enemy approach in battle:[/b] AI torpedo craft charge only targets they can actually reach, AI cruisers no longer sail into your fire when their guns cannot hurt your belt anyway, and ships on the approach zigzag to throw off your gunnery - your shooting at a turning ship gets harder, theirs too, so they straighten up once their own guns matter.
[/list]

Iron Admiral is an extended edition of the Dreadnought Improvement Project "The Great Game" 1.3.5: the same map, nations and data, plus the DIP_GG_Fixes add-on that changes how the AI fights and runs its campaign where CSV files cannot reach. One-click install, full uninstall, source code in the package. Current add-on: DIP_GG_Fixes 1.11.51.

[h2]Battle: an enemy that fights like a navy[/h2]
[list]
[*][b]Torpedo charges that make sense.[/b] AI destroyers, torpedo boats and light cruisers go for your battleships and heavy cruisers to deliver torpedoes. The start distance follows from their torpedo range and speed advantage, not from a constant: a ship with 850 m torpedoes no longer charges from 9 km. Out of torpedoes or badly hit, it withdraws; slower than its pursuers, it fights to the end.
[*][b]Capital ships charge too.[/b] An AI battleship or cruiser with torpedoes and a speed edge closes in; outranged or unable to penetrate your armour, it shortens the distance instead of dying at the edge of range.
[*][b]No more free kills.[/b] Any AI division that cannot outrun your pursuit stops fleeing and turns to fight. A faster one keeps running, as it should.
[*][b]Cripples don't slow the fleet.[/b] Slow, damaged ships are detached and retreat on their own; the healthy ones fight at full speed.
[*][b]Target selection like a real bridge[/b] (AI and player, when no manual target is set): guns engage the most damaged enemy in range, battleship main batteries aim at the heaviest enemy calibre, secondaries watch the nearest torpedo threat. A manual order always takes precedence.
[*][b]National battle doctrines[/b] (15 nations in an editable CSV): the Royal Navy keeps range and line, Japan charges with torpedoes from 16 km and accepts losses, Italy fights only with an advantage, Germany covers the turn-away with torpedo flotillas. Based on the Grand Fleet Battle Orders, the USN War Instructions and the Kaisen Yomurei literature.
[/list]

[h2]Your ships: less micromanagement[/h2]
[list]
[*]A TB/DD/CL division set as [b]Scout[/b] runs the torpedo charge itself, like the AI, and comes back when the torpedoes are gone or things get hot.
[*][b]Torpedoes do not run into friendly ships:[/b] when a friendly hull is in the lane to the target, the salvo waits until the lane is clear. Your own torpedo-mode change takes precedence.
[*]The automatic target selection above works for your ships as well.
[/list]

[h2]The crew matters[/h2]
[list]
[*][b]Experience really counts.[/b] The six training levels (cadets to veterans) are rescaled non-linearly: veterans up to +100% in accuracy, aiming and damage control with reload twice as fast; cadets down to -50% and reload twice as slow. Middle levels change little, the extremes a lot.
[*][b]Surrender depends on training:[/b] a green crew gives up the ship at 70% crew strength, an elite one fights down to 40% (vanilla: 55% for everybody).
[*][b]Panic.[/b] Inexperienced crews on badly damaged ships may break and leave the line - AI and player alike. A well-trained fleet is not only more accurate, it is a fleet that does not run.
[*][b]Prizes.[/b] A ship that surrenders falls to the winner if the winner still has working ships on the field: it goes to repair with a green crew, and its design joins your design list. Like Nebogatov's squadron after Tsushima.
[/list]

[h2]Campaign: an AI that does not give up[/h2]
[list]
[*]An AI nation that cannot afford a battleship builds cruisers instead of keeping its yards empty.
[*][b]Balance of power:[/b] when your fleet outgrows the strongest rival by half, AI nations receive emergency naval appropriations. Dominance has a price.
[*]Construction watch: if the game skips a nation's construction progress in a turn, the add-on does it instead.
[*]Your designs go automatically to the shared design pool, so the AI can build from them.
[*][b]Fleets change hands.[/b] When a nation loses its last port, or is dissolved, its ships in the fallen ports pass to the new controller instead of vanishing on the scrap heap - take Port Arthur and you take the fleet inside it. Works for you and against you.
[*]The post-war crisis hurts but no longer traps nations in an endless spiral: a nation at zero budget vanishes only after 24 months of crisis.
[*][b]Ships are built in classes.[/b] Prototypes cost more, follow-on ships get cheaper and faster, idle yards bid lower - for every nation.
[*][b]Money has a price.[/b] Inflation, credit ratings and interest follow loans, printing and windfalls; a treasury panel (F8) shows where your money goes.
[/list]

[h2]A world that lives[/h2]
[list]
[*]Sovereign minor nations (Latin America, Persia, Siam, the Balkans, Scandinavia and more) get their territories back, form alliances, buy ships and take part in wars as in vanilla; in the original DIP most of them existed only on paper.
[*]Minors have stronger province defence and doubled port capacity in the warship trade, so they order ships from you more often.
[*]Diplomacy with consequences: improvements x1.5, worsening x4, for the player and the AI.
[*]Revolts and internal instability more frequent, stronger and geographically varied.
[/list]

[h2]History in numbers[/h2]
[list]
[*][b]Penetration from physics, not from a slider:[/b] curves for 21 calibres and 5 gun generations rebuilt with the De Marre formula and calibrated on six navweaps.com tables (error 0.5-13%), the 16-21" calibres anchored on Okun's tables. Small guns no longer scratch a battleship's belt.
[*]Barrel weights from a power law calibrated on the 16"/50 Mk 7, 15"/42 Mk I and 46 cm Type 94.
[*][b]The naval treaties modelled for the first time:[/b] 35,000 t from the 1919 hull tech, 45,000 t from 1936, limits lapse in 1940.
[*]After the treaties, design freedom up to 240,000 t for the super-battleship tier (DIP: 78,500-130,000 t). The ship H-44 could not be is buildable here, by you and by the AI.
[*]Hulls that could be researched but never built in DIP are fixed (29 Modern Battleship variants, 23 earlier battleships, 5 destroyers, a corrupted CSV row that blocked the unlocks).
[*]Radar that works at night and in bad weather (three techs, 1935-1945). Long Lance: +100% range instead of +40%.
[*]Long-range accuracy grows x2.6 between 1920 and 1945 through the stereo and radar rangefinders, following W.J. Jurens' data on US Navy battleship gunnery (range dispersion down ~66%).
[*][b]National research profiles.[/b] Each great power researches at its own pace in its own fields, from the historical record: Germany in armour, optics and survivability, Britain in heavy guns, turbines and anti-submarine warfare, the USA in fire control, shells and shipbuilding, Japan in torpedoes and night fighting, Italy in speed, France in destroyers and quadruple turrets - and each pays for its historical weaknesses (Jutland shells, the Mk 14 torpedo, Richelieu's 380mm). -10% to +50% per category, visible in the research window, for you and for the AI.
[*][b]Treasury crisis.[/b] A great power whose cash stays below zero for six months must act, as governments did between 1890 and 1945: a war loan (debt cleared and money at once, repaid over years with interest set by the credit rating), money printing (cash at once, then inflation and unrest) or fleet cuts (no new battleships for a while, cheaper upkeep). The AI chooses by its situation - loan at war, cuts in peace, printing when a loan did not help; you get the choice in a dialog. The state survives save and load. A bankrupt AI power orders no battleships at all and builds cruisers, destroyers and torpedo boats instead - Britain at -188M no longer wastes years on semi-dreadnoughts that never leave the drawing board.
[*]Research 20% faster, technology cost x10, AI research priorities x1.5, a new profile for the Byzantine Empire (heavy armour, the biggest calibres, fast cruisers).
[/list]

[h2]Stability and transparency[/h2]
[list]
[*]The add-on catches two bugs in the game's own code that aborted turn processing in DIP ("An item with the same key has already been added").
[*][b]Update check and one-click update.[/b] At start the add-on compares the installed version with the GitHub repository and shows a message when a newer build exists - one HTTPS request, nothing sent. The message has a Download button: only after you click it the Mods package is fetched from the GitHub release, verified (SHA-256) and installed; restart the game afterwards. Switches: CheckForUpdates, UpdateDownloadButton.
[*]Every add-on feature has a switch and values in UserData\MelonPreferences.cfg [DIP_GG_Fixes]; doctrines in Mods\battleDoctrines.csv, research profiles in Mods\techBonuses.csv. Nothing is forced.
[*]Every change is documented with reasoning, sources and test status in CHANGELOG_EN.txt (74 sections). Source code included (Apache 2.0). Extension by Klaudiusz Ledwoch (Steam: Klod76). Built on DIP (Brother Munro & team, Apache 2.0), TweaksAndFixes (NathanKell, MIT) and MelonLoader (LavaGang, Apache 2.0).
[/list]

[*][b]Traktaty morskie po raz pierwszy zamodelowane:[/b] 35 000 t od technologii kadłuba z 1919 r., 45 000 t od 1936 r., koniec ograniczeń w 1940 r.
[*]Po traktatach wolność projektowania do 240 000 t dla warstwy superpancerników (DIP: 78 500-130 000 t). Okręt, którym nie mógł być H-44, tu da się zbudować - Tobie i AI.

[*][b]Narodowe profile badań.[/b] Każde mocarstwo bada w swoim tempie w swoich dziedzinach, zgodnie z historią: Niemcy pancerz, optykę i odporność, Brytania ciężkie działa, turbiny i ZOP, USA kierowanie ogniem, pociski i stocznie, Japonia torpedy i walkę nocną, Włochy prędkość, Francja niszczyciele i wieże czterodziałowe - i każde płaci za swoje historyczne słabości (pociski spod Jutlandii, torpeda Mk 14, 380 mm Richelieu). [*][b]Kryzys skarbu.[/b] Mocarstwo, którego kasa przez sześć miesięcy jest poniżej zera, musi działać tak, jak rządy w latach 1890-1945: pożyczka wojenna (dług skasowany i pieniądze od razu, spłata latami z odsetkami zależnymi od ratingu), dodruk pieniądza (gotówka od razu, potem inflacja i niepokoje) albo cięcia floty (przez pewien czas bez nowych pancerników, tańsze utrzymanie). AI wybiera według sytuacji - pożyczka w wojnie, cięcia w pokoju, dodruk gdy pożyczka nie pomogła; Ty wybierasz w oknie. Stan przeżywa zapis i wczytanie gry. Zbankrutowane mocarstwo AI nie zamawia w ogóle pancerników, tylko krążowniki, niszczyciele i torpedowce - Brytania z -188 mln nie traci już lat na półdrednoty, które nigdy nie schodzą z deski.
[*]Badania o 20% szybsze, koszt technologii x10, priorytety badań AI x1,5, nowy profil Bizancjum (ciężki pancerz, największe kalibry, szybkie krążowniki).
[/list]
